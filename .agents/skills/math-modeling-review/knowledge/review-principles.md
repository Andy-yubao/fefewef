# Review Principles

Apply these priorities in order:

```text
specific > generic
evidence > impression
actionable > descriptive
priority > exhaustive
clarity > decoration
competition outcome > academic verbosity
```

## Evidence discipline

- Treat the problem statement as the source of task requirements and the final rendered paper as the source of what judges see.
- Anchor findings to a page, section, equation, figure, table, or identifiable passage.
- Quote sparingly; a precise paraphrase plus location is normally enough.
- Separate observed fact (`The summary reports no numerical result`) from inference (`A judge may not see what the model achieved`).
- If evidence is inaccessible, contradictory, or ambiguous, say so and lower confidence. Never complete a missing derivation or result on the authors' behalf.

## Competition-centered judgment

Prioritize whether the team answered every requested task, communicates the main contribution rapidly, connects models into a solution, supports important claims, and demonstrates that the recommendation is not fragile. Technical sophistication does not compensate for an unanswered requirement or an invisible conclusion.

Review depth should follow consequence. Spend more attention on choices and uncertainties that could reverse a ranking, invalidate a recommendation, or change task coverage than on routine mechanics.

## Minimal viable repair

Recommend the smallest edit or analysis that closes the demonstrated gap. Examples include adding one transition that names an upstream output, inserting a results sentence with an existing numerical value, narrowing an overclaim, or perturbing the two parameters that drive the final decision. Do not prescribe a wholesale rewrite when a local repair is sufficient.
